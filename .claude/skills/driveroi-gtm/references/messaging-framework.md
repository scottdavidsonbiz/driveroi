## SPICED Discovery Framework

Used for discovery call prep and debrief. Each element captures critical information.

### Framework Elements

| Element | Definition | Questions |
|---------|-----------|-----------|
| **S — Situation** | Current state. What does the company do? Revenue range, team size, funding stage, sales motion, tech stack. | How many people are on your sales team? What's your current tech stack? How much revenue did you do last year? |
| **P — Pain** | What's broken? Where are they bleeding money/time? What keeps the founder up at night? | Where are deals getting stuck? How long is your sales cycle? How much time do you spend on manual reporting? |
| **I — Impact** | What does the pain cost them? Revenue left on the table, time wasted, investor confidence eroded, inability to hire/scale. | How many deals do you lose to stalling? What's the economic impact? How does this affect hiring? |
| **C — Critical Event** | What's the forcing function? Board meeting, fundraise, new hire starting, investor pressure, competitor move. | When do you need this solved? What's driving urgency? |
| **E — Decision** | Who decides? Budget holder, champion, influencer, blocker. What's the buying process? | Who has final sign-off? Who else needs to be involved? What's the approval process? |
| **D — Decision Criteria** | What will they evaluate us on? Speed, cost, expertise, cultural fit, deliverables. | How will you measure success? What matters most to you: speed, cost, or coverage? |

### Discovery Call Best Practices

#### StoryBrand Call Flow

The discovery call follows the StoryBrand arc: the prospect is the hero with a problem, you're the guide with empathy and a plan.

**Opening (2 min):** Name their internal problem. Don't pitch.
- "Most founders I talk to are in a similar spot: they've got tools, maybe a rep or two, but pipeline still feels unpredictable. Is that close to what you're seeing?"
- This positions you as empathetic guide from the first sentence. Let them talk.

**Discovery (15-20 min):** Use SPICED to dig into their story.
- Surface the external problem (what's broken)
- Surface the internal problem (how it makes them feel: overwhelmed, behind, frustrated)
- Quantify the impact (cost of inaction)
- Find the critical event (what forces a decision)

**The Plan (3-5 min):** Only after they've described their problem.
- "Here's typically what we'd do. First, a two-week audit: we look at your pipeline, process, and stack. You get a report with specific findings. If it makes sense, we build the system in weeks 3-8. Then your team runs it."
- Three clear steps. Reduces anxiety.

**Stakes (1-2 min):** Gently name the failure path.
- "The alternative is usually one of two things: keep running the current process and hope it scales, or hire a full-time ops person at $120-130K who takes 6 months to ramp."

**Close (2 min):** Clear next step.
- "I'll send a summary by [day]. If it makes sense, we can talk about the audit sprint."

#### Tactical Rules

1. **Lead with the prospect's world, not your pitch** — Ask about their pipeline, team, and process. Listen 70% of the time.

2. **Listen for the equity vs retainer objection early** — Address with sprint model: "We don't do open-ended retainers. Two-week audit, clear findings, you decide next steps."

3. **Get specific** — "How many deals are in your pipeline right now?" not "Tell me about your pipeline."

4. **Identify the critical event** — That's your urgency lever. Board meeting? Fundraise? New hire? Use it.

5. **Always end with a clear next step and timeline** — "I'll send a summary by Wednesday. We can talk Thursday if that works."

6. **After the call: debrief within 24 hours** — Create follow-up package within 48 hours.

### Objection Handlers

#### "We can't afford a consultant right now"

**Response:**
"Totally fair. That's why we don't do open-ended retainers. Our audit sprint is 2 weeks — you get a full pipeline diagnostic and action plan. If the ROI isn't obvious, you stop there. No long-term commitment."

#### "We'd prefer equity over cash payment"

**Response:**
"We've structured engagements both ways. For a first engagement, a short paid sprint lets both sides test fit without long-term commitment. If we prove the value, we can discuss creative structures for ongoing work."

#### "We already have a CRM / HubSpot / Salesforce"

**Response:**
"Great — we're not replacing your tools, we're making them work. Most companies have the tech but not the architecture. We build the pipeline stages, exit criteria, and dashboards on top of what you already have."

#### "What makes you different from a fractional CRO?"

**Response:**
"A fractional CRO gives you strategy and sits in your leadership meetings. We give you strategy AND we build it. Pipeline architecture, sequences, dashboards, playbooks — all delivered and working in weeks, not months."

## Writing Style Guide

### Voice & Tone

- **First person plural** ("we") for DriveROI
- **Second person** ("you") for prospect
- **Short sentences.** Active voice. No passive constructions.
- **Be specific** — say "pipeline architecture with deal stage governance" not "CRM optimization"
- **Metrics when available** — "70% fewer reporting requests" not "significantly reduced reporting"

### Words to Use

- build
- deliver
- sprint
- pipeline
- conversion
- velocity
- repeatable
- stage
- forecast
- governance
- cadence

### Words to Avoid

- synergy
- leverage (as verb)
- best-in-class
- world-class
- cutting-edge
- robust
- innovative
- solution

### Examples

**Do:**
"We build pipeline architecture with clear exit criteria so your team knows exactly when a deal moves forward."

**Don't:**
"We leverage best-in-class CRM optimization to synergize your sales process."

**Do:**
"You'll have a board-ready dashboard showing pipeline coverage by segment and conversion velocity."

**Don't:**
"We provide cutting-edge analytics insights utilizing advanced reporting mechanisms."
